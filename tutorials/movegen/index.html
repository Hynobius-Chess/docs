    Move Generator¶

    Describe in One Sentence¶

    A move generator is responsible for generating every possible move from a position. It is one of the most basic and important parts of a chess engine.

    Introduction¶

    Please read Chess Engine first before continuing.

    Move generator is a critical component of an engine. It is responsible for generating every possible move from a position. This "function" may be called millions or billions of times while the engine is searching.

    Application¶

    When a chess engine wants to choose a move, it does not only look at the current position. It also looks ahead.

    For example, the engine may ask:

    1. What moves can I play now?
    2. After I play one move, what moves can my opponent play?
    3. After my opponent replies, what moves can I play next?
    4. Which final positions are good or bad for me?

    To answer these questions, the engine needs the move generator again and again. Search depends on move generation.

    If the move generator is wrong, the whole engine becomes wrong. If it misses a legal move, the engine may never find a winning idea. If it creates an illegal move, the engine may search a position that can never happen in a real game.

    So move generation must be both:

    1. Correct: every legal move is generated, and no illegal move is generated.
    2. Fast: it must do this many times per second.

    What Is a Move?¶

    To humans, a move looks simple:

    e2e4

    This means a piece moves from e2 to e4.

    But inside a chess engine, a move usually needs more information:

    1. The starting square.
    2. The target square.
    3. The moving piece.
    4. The captured piece, if there is one.
    5. Special move information, such as promotion, castling, or en passant.

    For example, e7e8q means a pawn moves from e7 to e8 and promotes to a queen.

    The move generator's job is to create a list of these moves.

    Pseudo-Legal Moves and Legal Moves¶

    Many engines split move generation into two steps:

    1. Generate pseudo-legal moves.
    2. Remove moves that are not actually legal.

    A pseudo-legal move is a move that follows the piece's movement rule, but may still leave the king in check.

    For example, imagine your king is on e1, and an enemy rook is attacking the e file. If you move a pinned piece away, your king may become exposed. The piece itself moved in a normal way, but the move is illegal because your king is left in check.

    So pseudo-legal move generation asks:

    Can this piece move like that?

    Legal move generation asks:

    After this move, is my king still safe?

    This difference is very important.

    How Each Piece Generates Moves¶

    Each type of piece has its own movement rules.

    Pawns¶

    Pawns are the most complicated normal piece because they have many special rules.

    A pawn may:

    1. Move one square forward if the square is empty.
    2. Move two squares forward from its starting rank if both squares are empty.
    3. Capture one square diagonally forward.
    4. Promote when it reaches the last rank.
    5. Capture en passant in special positions.

    Because pawns move differently for White and Black, the engine must handle the two colors carefully.

    Knights¶

    Knights are easier. A knight jumps in an L shape.

    It can jump over other pieces, so the engine only needs to check the target square:

    1. If the target square is empty, the knight can move there.
    2. If the target square has an enemy piece, the knight can capture it.
    3. If the target square has a friendly piece, the knight cannot move there.

    Bishops, Rooks, and Queens¶

    Bishops, rooks, and queens are sliding pieces.

    This means they move in a direction until something blocks them.

    A bishop slides diagonally. A rook slides horizontally and vertically. A queen can slide in all of those directions.

    For each direction, the engine keeps walking square by square:

    1. If the square is empty, add the move and continue.
    2. If the square has an enemy piece, add the capture and stop.
    3. If the square has a friendly piece, stop.

    King¶

    The king usually moves one square in any direction.

    However, the king is special because it can never move into check. Castling is also handled as a special king move.

    For castling, the engine must check several things:

    1. The king and rook have not moved before.
    2. The squares between them are empty.
    3. The king is not currently in check.
    4. The king does not pass through check.
    5. The king does not end in check.

    Why Move Generation Must Be Fast¶

    A chess engine search grows very quickly.

    If one position has about 35 legal moves, then looking only 1 move ahead gives about 35 positions. Looking 2 moves ahead gives about:

    35 * 35 = 1225 positions

    Looking 3 moves ahead gives about:

    35 * 35 * 35 = 42875 positions

    This number grows extremely fast.

    Because of this, even a small improvement in move generation speed can make the engine search much more deeply.

    A Simple Move Generation Flow¶

    A simple engine may generate moves like this:

    generate_moves(position):
    moves = []

    for each piece of the side to move:
        generate moves for that piece
        add them to moves

    legal_moves = []

    for each move in moves:
        make the move
        if own king is not in check:
            add move to legal_moves
        undo the move

    return legal_moves

    This is not always the fastest design, but it is easy to understand.

    The important idea is:

    First create possible moves, then keep only the legal ones.

    Common Bugs in Move Generation¶

    Move generation is easy to describe, but hard to get perfectly correct.

    Common bugs include:

    1. Forgetting promotion moves.
    2. Allowing castling through check.
    3. Handling en passant incorrectly.
    4. Forgetting that pinned pieces may not be able to move.
    5. Generating moves that capture friendly pieces.
    6. Moving a sliding piece through another piece.
    7. Forgetting to update castling rights after a king or rook moves.

    These bugs can be hard to see by playing normal games, so engines usually use tests.

    Perft: Testing Move Generation¶

    perft is a common test for move generators.

    It means "performance test", but it is mostly used to test correctness.

    The idea is simple:

    Count how many legal positions can be reached after N plies.

    A ply means one move by one side. For example, White plays e2e4; that is one ply. Then Black replies e7e5; that is another ply.

    If your engine's perft number is different from the known correct number, your move generator has a bug.

    For example, from the starting position:

    perft(1) = 20
perft(2) = 400
perft(3) = 8902

    These numbers are useful because they quickly tell you whether your legal move generation is correct.

    Summary¶

    Move generation is the part of a chess engine that lists all legal moves from a position.

    It sounds simple, but it must understand every chess rule: normal moves, captures, checks, castling, promotion, and en passant.

    The search function asks the move generator for moves again and again. Because of that, move generation must be correct and fast.

    In short:

    No correct movegen, no correct chess engine.
